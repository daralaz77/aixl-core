# blind30 (120 pairs) — pre-registration, written 2026-11-11 BEFORE the pairs exist
Purpose: a PAIRED, FRESH test of two comparator rules written after reading blind28 and blind29 (uncommitted in the aixl-core working tree; tests in tests/test_semantic_blind30.py): (1) 'each / every / cada / todo' and 'all / todos' on the SUBJECT are proven equal when nothing is counted, summed, excepted or ordered, and exactly one plural carried by the quantified subject is not a difference; (2) a passive with an explicit agent AND a modal ('must be approved by the auditor') is rewritten to the active sentence. On the spent sets they gave 6 more proven equivalents (blind28 + blind29) and no new false accept across 880 pairs: DEVELOPMENT evidence only. A first, broader version of rule (1) accepted 2 wrong pairs on blind24 and was narrowed before this test; that is disclosed here.
Design: the SAME fresh pairs go through the OLD comparator (git tag v0.7.0, extracted unchanged) and the NEW comparator (frozen working tree), and through two blind Claude judges (Opus, Sonnet; arbiter_rules_v1.txt verbatim). Paired, so any OLD/NEW difference comes from the changes, not the sample.
Authors: three fresh agents, no tools except Write to one path each: Opus (T001-T040), Sonnet (U001-U040), Haiku (V001-V040); nothing about AIXL, blind28/29 or any result. The same ten in-vocabulary constructs and label definitions as blind28/29, but the two targeted constructs are deliberately OVER-SAMPLED: word_order_voice and quantifier_all_each_any get 8 pairs each per author, the other eight constructs 3 pairs each (8+8+24 = 40). Total 120; about 50% EQUIVALENT, 35% NOT_EQUIVALENT, 15% UNDECIDABLE; es / en / pt mixed. Labels are the authors' and are never corrected; G1 counts UNDECIDABLE as non-equivalent.
Frozen: SHA-256 of the NEW and OLD code files go into CODE_FREEZE.json, the author files into MANIFEST.json, before the judges run. One run; the evaluator refuses a second run; the result file is written with an editor tool.

Metrics (fixed now): per system (OLD, NEW, NEW AND both judges SAME, judges alone): false accepts over the NOT_EQUIVALENT-labelled pairs (UNDECIDABLE included), proven-EQUIVALENT over the EQUIVALENT-labelled pairs, wrong NOT_EQUIVALENT over the EQUIVALENT-labelled pairs. Paired: pairs newly proven by NEW, pairs lost, new false accepts, false accepts removed. Everything is reported overall, for the two TARGETED constructs together, and for the EIGHT OTHER constructs together.
Decision rules (fixed now):
  R-recall: on the two targeted constructs, NEW proves at least 5 more EQUIVALENT-labelled pairs than OLD (net, paired) AND adds 0 new false accepts overall.
  R-noharm: on the eight other constructs NEW adds 0 new false accepts and loses at most 1 proven pair.
  R-safety: NEW has no new false accept anywhere; any false accept that NEW has and OLD did not is reported FIRST and counts against the change.
  G1 for NEW (reported, decides any safety claim): NEW false accepts <= 3% of the non-EQUIVALENT-labelled pairs (about 2 of 60 here is 3.3%, so the bound used is <= 1 of ~58); R1-new (NEW AND both judges) reported with the vacuity floor of 10 proven pairs.
Claim policy: if R-recall and R-noharm hold: "on N fresh sentences of the comparator's constructs, the changed comparator proved k more equivalent pairs than v0.7.0 in the targeted constructs, with no new false accept". If a rule fails it is reported as failed and the failing rule is reverted or narrowed. Never a generalisation claim (in-distribution constructs, model authors, over-sampled targets, small sample); the overall gain is inflated by the over-sampling and the 'other constructs' line is the honest check.
Expectations written beforehand: NEW gains 3-7 proven pairs in the targeted constructs (the passive rule needs a modal and a recognised verb; the quantifier rule needs a subject quantifier with no number or order marker, so only a fraction of fresh sentences qualify) and R-recall has about even odds; 0-1 new false accepts (the rules were built with guards but each set found a new hole); OLD and NEW each have 1-4 false accepts of ~58; the two judges 7-12% false SAME, mostly UNDECIDABLE-labelled.
Honest limits: tuned constructs and rules written after reading the previous sets; model authors and labels; 120 pairs; two Claude judges that agree on almost everything; over-sampling of the targets.
